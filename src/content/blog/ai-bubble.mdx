## The Efficiency Gap

Modern GPUs remain unmatched for large-batch training workloads. Their architecture is optimized for massive parallel throughput, and they are the foundation of contemporary model development.

Inference, however, is governed by a different set of constraints.

As models move into production, **latency, determinism, memory locality, and energy efficiency** dominate cost and performance. Token-by-token generation (especially at batch sizes of one) maps poorly onto throughput-optimized hardware. GPUs can perform inference effectively, but often at a power and cost profile that does not scale cleanly with demand. [3]

## Market Consolidation

**Jonathan Ross**, a former Google engineer who contributed to early TPU development, co-founded **Groq** to address inference latency and efficiency at the architectural level.

Rather than acquiring Groq outright, Nvidia entered a **large-scale licensing and talent acquisition agreement reportedly valued at approximately $20 billion**, securing access to key inference IP while bringing a substantial portion of Groq’s senior engineering leadership in-house. Groq continues to operate independently, including its cloud offering, but the transaction materially reduced competitive pressure on Nvidia in inference-specific hardware. [4]

This pattern (licensing, selective acqui-hire, and ecosystem absorption) has emerged as a recurring consolidation mechanism across AI infrastructure.

### The CUDA Lock-In

Hardware alone is not the moat. Software is.

The modern ML ecosystem—frameworks, kernels, compilers, and institutional expertise—is deeply optimized for CUDA. Migrating inference workloads to non-CUDA platforms often requires rewriting low-level kernels, retraining teams, and accepting tooling risk.

## Conclusion

The bubble is not AI demand. It is **the cost structure used to satisfy that demand**.

Venture capital and strategic investment are currently absorbing the inefficiencies of general-purpose hardware performing inference workloads it was not architected to optimize. As inference eclipses training in both volume and operating cost, inference-native silicon is no longer optional.

It is the only configuration where the math closes long-term.
